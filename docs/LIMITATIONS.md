# Limitations

*Plain-language summary: what this benchmark cannot tell you. Read it before relying on any result. Each point is specific so that reviewers can check it.*

1. **"Barren" mostly means "no deposit on record."** Most barren intrusions have no positive evidence of being explored and found empty. They are labeled barren only because no Ni-Cu-PGE deposit appears in the USGS lists, MRDS, or the curated list. Some will host undiscovered deposits. `label_confidence` marks this, and a sensitivity analysis drops LOW-confidence barren intrusions.
2. **Fertile labels rely on a curated list.** The automatic sources found few fertile intrusions outside North America, because MRDS barely covers some regions (for example China). The curated list was drafted from the deposit literature and reviewed by the authors. Every fertile and weakly mineralized intrusion cites a published source, except Agnew and Monts de Cristal, which are flagged in the curated file. For Kongsberg, Modum and Haines, the host relationship rests on a mine within 10 km plus the authors' judgment.
3. **About half of the mafic-ultramafic samples cannot be tied to an intrusion.** GEOROC often records only a region. Those samples are not in the benchmark, and major ore-bearing intrusions such as Voisey's Bay, Sudbury, Kalatongke, Tamarack and Kabanga have no named mafic-ultramafic samples in GEOROC, and Jinchuan enters only through samples recorded as 'Jinchuan area'.
4. **Very few liquid-like samples from fertile intrusions.** Chilled margins and fine-grained equivalents of ore-bearing intrusions are rare in GEOROC, so the fairest comparison (on liquids) is mostly "insufficient data" in v0.1.
5. **Sample types come from rules.** The tags use the text and chemistry GEOROC records. Many samples have no texture information and stay UNRESOLVED. The authors spot-checked eleven samples, but a systematic expert review of the tags is deferred to v0.2, so no agreement rate is reported yet. The rules are not a petrographic description.
6. **Platinum-group element data are sparse and uneven.** PGE ratios can be scored for only about a dozen fertile intrusions, so their confidence intervals are wide. Two source references had entered PGE values in ppb as ppm; after the authors checked the original papers these were converted. MgO values below 0.5 wt% in rocks other than anorthosite were blanked as source errors.
7. **Fertile intrusions are often sampled near ore.** Many fertile samples are sulfide-bearing cumulates from mineralized zones. This favors indices that detect sulfide itself, and penalizes depletion indices designed for sulfide-poor melts. That is a finding about the open data, not about the indices' value in their intended setting.
8. **One deposit type, broadly defined.** Reef-type PGE deposits (e.g. Bushveld, Stillwater, Great Dyke) are counted as fertile alongside sulfide-rich Ni-Cu deposits. Komatiite lavas, ophiolites, Ural-Alaskan-type Pt placers and laterites are out of scope.
9. **Index directions follow the source hypotheses.** Four of the twelve indices (Pt/Pd, Ni/Cu, Zr/Y and the two-sided Cu/Pd form) are exploratory: their direction was fixed by the authors before scoring rather than taken from a published fertility test.
10. **AUC measures ranking, not a usable threshold.** A good AUC does not give an exploration cut-off value.
11. **Coordinates are approximate.** GEOROC coordinates can be rounded or generalized, which affects the 10 km matching with deposit records.
